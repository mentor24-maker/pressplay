City council approves $210M transit expansion after marathon session

The Denver Ledger (demo)·Updated 2h ago· Viral 92

The story in 30 seconds

After a six-hour session and more than sixty public comments, the city council voted 7–2 to approve a $210 million transit package — the largest single transit investment in the city's history.

  • Funds three new rapid-bus corridors connecting the north and west sides to downtown.
  • Extends light-rail service east by 4.2 miles with three new stations.
  • Construction begins next spring; the first corridor opens within 18 months.
  • Paid for through a mix of federal grants, bonds, and an existing sales-tax allocation — no new taxes.

Why it matters

Transit ridership is still recovering from pandemic-era lows, and this vote is a bet that faster, more frequent service — not fare cuts — is what brings riders back. Cities nationwide are watching the same question.

Supporters say

Rapid-bus corridors are the fastest, cheapest way to serve neighborhoods light rail will never reach — and construction jobs start immediately.

Skeptics say

Ridership projections have missed before; two council members argued the funds should shore up existing routes first.
